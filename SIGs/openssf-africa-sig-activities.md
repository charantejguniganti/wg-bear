## 1. Executive Summary

The OpenSSF Africa SIG is a regional community initiative operating under the OpenSSF BEAR Working Group. Its focus is to strengthen engagement with open-source software security across African communities through outreach, education, collaboration, contributor development, and participation in the broader OpenSSF ecosystem.

The available records describe the initiative's development, community engagement activities, collaboration with educational institutions, conference participation, research ambitions, and efforts to establish a sustainable organizational structure.

This report consolidates those records into an organized account of activities and milestones. It explicitly distinguishes:

- Documented completed activities
- Ongoing activities
- Proposals and plans for future work
- Items requiring further confirmation from contributors

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This document is an evidence-based draft. Sections marked **"Awaiting confirmation"** must be verified with the relevant SIG contributors or maintainers before this document is considered final.

## 2. OpenSSF BEAR Working Group Background

The BEAR Working Group (Belonging, Empowerment, Allyship, and Representation), formerly known as the DEI Working Group, was formed in **December 2023**.

Its stated purpose is to improve representation and strengthen cybersecurity workforce effectiveness.

**Mission statement** (from [README.md](../README.md)):

> *"To foster a more diverse and inclusive cybersecurity workforce by implementing strategies to increase representation and enhance effectiveness through advocacy, collaboration, and tailored initiatives."*

The 2024 and 2025 BEAR meeting notes document the group's work across several areas:

| Activity area | Description |
|---|---|
| Community office hours | Monthly topic-centred panel discussions |
| Newcomer participation | Contributor onboarding and welcome processes |
| Mentorship | Planning and coordination for mentorship opportunities |
| Community documentation | Maintaining documentation and communication resources |
| Cross-community collaboration | Working with other open-source communities and foundations |
| Conference participation | Hosting panel discussions and talks at industry events |

## 3. OpenSSF Africa SIG — Formation and Development

### 3.1 Proposal and Community Discussion

During **November 2025**, BEAR meeting discussions explored the establishment of an Open Source & Security Africa Special Interest Group.

The proposal described several objectives:

- Building an interconnected network of open-source contributors and security professionals across Africa
- Providing community-driven education on secure development and software supply-chain security
- Partnering with local and regional communities across Africa
- Establishing country chapters that could contribute to global open-source projects and security standards

### 3.2 Approval Process

The November and December 2025 BEAR meeting notes document the proposal's progression through community review, feedback, and a formal voting process.

The **December 16, 2025** meeting notes record that there were **no votes against** the OSSAfrica SIG proposal and document subsequent next steps.

> [!NOTE]
> The meeting notes record the absence of negative votes. The specific approval threshold, quorum, and formal resolution language should be confirmed against the actual meeting minutes before this record is treated as a definitive account of the approval.

**Sources:**
- BEAR meeting notes — November 4, November 18, December 2, and December 16, 2025
- [GitHub Issue #20 — Africa SIG proposal](https://github.com/ossf/wg-bear/issues/20)

### 4.2 University of Lagos (UNILAG) Collaboration

The Africa SIG update presentation describes the **NitHub Cybersecurity Talent Showcase** as the capstone event concluding a **two-month beginner cybersecurity training programme** at the University of Lagos.

#### Programme content

Students presented work across three technical domains:

| Domain | Description |
|---|---|
| Security Operations Center (SOC) | Monitoring, detection, and incident response concepts |
| Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing (VAPT) | Identifying and assessing system vulnerabilities |
| API security testing | Testing and securing application programming interfaces |

#### OpenSSF involvement

The presentation records the following OpenSSF contributions to the showcase:

- A **virtual introduction to OpenSSF** delivered by **David Wheeler**
- Guidance from **Ejiro** on free educational courses available through OpenSSF
- Participation by **Harmony** and **Ijeoma** in facilitating the collaboration
- Some students joining the **OpenSSF Slack community** following the event

**Current status:** Activity described in presentation.

> [!NOTE]
> **Evidence to add:** Date of the showcase; number of students trained and participating; event or programme publication; confirmation of continued student participation; any follow-up projects or contributions.

## 5. Research and Ecosystem Development

### 5.1 African Open-Source Cybersecurity Research Proposal

The Africa SIG update presentation describes a proposed research initiative focused on the **African cybersecurity landscape** as it relates to open-source software.

#### Stated purpose

To understand whether African communities experience distinctive challenges in open-source security, and to identify ways in which OpenSSF could help address those challenges.

#### Funding status

The presentation records that the proposed research **required funding that OpenSSF could not provide** at the time of the presentation. It also records a suggestion from **David Wheeler** to explore alternative funding and collaboration opportunities.

**Current classification:** Research proposal. Funding availability, research partners, and implementation decisions require confirmation.

> [!NOTE]
> **Evidence to add:** Full research proposal document; research objectives and methodology; potential research partners; funding requirements and current status; any implementation decisions taken since the presentation.

## 8. Mentorship and Contributor Development

The 2024 and 2025 BEAR meeting notes discuss **mentorship** as a mechanism for helping newcomers develop the skills and experience needed to participate meaningfully in open-source security projects.

The 2025 BEAR discussions address:

- Identifying OpenSSF projects capable of hosting mentees
- Finding and engaging potential mentors
- Reviewing lessons from previous mentorship activities within the BEAR WG
- Considering programme budgets and timelines
- Connecting mentorship opportunities with technical projects and contributor development goals

The **December 2025** BEAR meeting notes identify mentorship as a **2026 priority**.

The BEAR WG Roadmap also documents the group's intention to enrol in the [LFX Mentorship Program](https://lfx.linuxfoundation.org/tools/mentorship/) and to seek funding to sponsor mentorships.

**Current classification:** Mentorship planning and priorities are documented at the BEAR WG level. Africa SIG-specific mentorship activities, mentees, mentors, and outcomes require separate confirmation.

## 11. Challenges and Opportunities

The available records identify several areas requiring continued attention.

### Funding and Sustainability

The proposed African open-source cybersecurity research initiative requires funding that was not available at the time of the presentation. Identifying suitable funding sources and sustainable support models is an ongoing area of work.

### Contributor Engagement

The presentation identifies outreach as an ongoing need. Continued contributor onboarding, accessible educational content, and clear entry points for participation could strengthen community growth.

### Governance and Coordination

The SIG structure and leadership processes require clear and publicly accessible documentation so that contributors understand responsibilities and how decisions are made.

### Regional Collaboration

The original SIG proposal emphasizes partnerships with local communities and the development of country-level participation. Progress on country chapters and regional partnerships requires further documentation.

### Mentorship

The BEAR meeting notes identify project availability, mentor participation, programme budgets, and timelines as open considerations. Africa SIG-specific mentorship arrangements have not yet been separately documented.
